Station app: handy commands
===========================

Setup helper ........... ~/station/app/bin/setup.sh   (callsign, location, radio, callbook, auto login)
Change settings ........ xed ~/station/config.ini      (or nano)
Health report .......... ~/station/app/bin/status.sh
Restart after changes .. systemctl --user restart station-app station-rigctld station-rigctld2
Is it running? ......... systemctl --user status station-app
Recent app messages .... journalctl --user -u station-app -n 50
Radio link messages .... journalctl --user -u station-rigctld -n 50   (the second radio's: station-rigctld2)
Reopen the display ..... Menu > Station display   (it also reopens by itself a few seconds after
                         it's closed, and restarts itself if its page stops answering for 5 minutes)
Close the display ...... Menu > Close station display   (or: systemctl --user stop station-display)
                         It opens again from Menu > Station display, or when the computer restarts.
                         Alt+F4 closes it too, but it reopens in a few seconds. To make Alt+F4 keep it
                         closed, add "keep_open = no" under [display] in config.ini.
Display messages ....... journalctl --user -u station-display -n 30
Back up right now ...... python3 ~/station/app/bin/backup.py
Backups ................ ~/station-backups  (logbook copy + ADIF, last 60 nights)
The logbook itself ..... ~/station/data/logbook.sqlite3
Automatic login ........ ~/station/app/bin/autologin.sh on    (or off)
Update the app ......... run install.sh from the new download; settings and the log are kept
Go back one version .... the previous version is kept in ~/station/app.old
Favourites ............. the Favourites button on the display opens them in the computer's
                         default web browser (Chromium or Firefox).
                         To change the list: python3 ~/station/app/station/favourites.py > ~/station/favourites.json
                         then edit that file and restart the app.
Browser bookmarks ...... if ~/station/ham-bookmarks.html is there, it has the same links.
                         Chromium: Bookmarks > Bookmark manager > the three dots > Import bookmarks
                         Firefox: Bookmarks > Manage Bookmarks > Import and Backup > Import Bookmarks from HTML
Map views .............. the buttons above the map: Flat map, Globe, Beam map. The display remembers
                         the one picked. On the flat map, looking up a station turns it into the globe
                         to show the path; closing the station card (the X, or Esc) brings it back.
                         Drag the globe to turn it. On the beam map, point anywhere to read the heading.
  no globe on look-up .. add "globe_on_lookup = no" under [display] in config.ini, then restart the app
CQ zones ............... the red numbers. Click one (or "CQ zones" on the map) for the zone's name,
                         its countries and prefixes, and which zones are in the log (for WAZ).
  hide them ............ "show_zones = no" under [display] in config.ini, then restart the app
Log a contact .......... "Log this contact" on the station card. The button turns green and says Logged,
                         and the My log tab gets a green dot. Pressing it again for the same station within
                         15 minutes asks first ("Log it again?"). Undo on the message takes it back out;
                         Add a note opens that contact's note.
Stats .................. the Stats button (or "See your stats" under My log): contacts, countries,
                         CQ zones, continents, stations, contacts per month, the furthest contact,
                         the latest new ones, the stations talked to most, contacts on this date in past
                         years, and every DXCC country with what's in the log. Point at a bar for its numbers.
  Stations ............. everyone in the log, with the first and last contact and how many: search by call,
                         name or country, and sort by latest, first met, most contacts, farthest or A to Z.
                         Pick one for every contact with them, and add or change a note on any contact.
                         "History and notes" on a station's card opens it there.
Spots .................. each spot shows the frequency and the sideband the station is on: USB or LSB (the
                         spotter's word for it if they gave one, otherwise the usual one for the band: LSB below
                         10 MHz, USB above). Point at a blue spot on the map to see who it is, where they are on
                         the dial and the spotter's note; click it to look them up. "Tune to" sets the radio to
                         that frequency and sideband.
Working split .......... a DX station listening somewhere else (the spot says UP 5, DN 2, QSX 14.210 ...) is marked
                         by its country: UP5 = listening 5 kHz up. "Tune to" then sets the radio to listen
                         on the DX station and transmit where they're listening (the near end of a range like
                         UP 5-10). The radio box shows SPLIT and the transmit frequency, red if that's on another
                         band. The station card's −1 kHz and +1 kHz move the transmit frequency, and Split off
                         ends it. Tuning to a station that isn't working split turns split off. It never sets a
                         transmit frequency that would put the signal outside the band (or, on SSB, outside the
                         voice part of the band), and it never transmits.
New ones ............... badges in the spot list: NEW = a country not in the log yet, ZONE = a new
                         CQ zone, BAND = a country in the log but not on that band yet. New countries
                         also get an amber ring on the maps.
DX cluster ............. spots come from [cluster] host in config.ini. If that node stops answering or
                         sending spots, the app moves to the next one under "backups" by itself, and tries
                         the first one again every hour. "station status" shows which one is in use.
Welcome banner ......... plays the first time the display opens after the computer starts. Click
                         the callsign at the top left to see it again; click anywhere to skip it.
  settings ............. under [display] in config.ini: "welcome = no" switches it off, and
                         welcome_text is the line under the callsign
Tester checklist ....... the link under the greeting opens the checklist in a browser window: where to
                         find each part of the display, how to try it, Found it / Tried it, Like it /
                         Needs a change, and notes. Answers are kept in ~/station/data/checklist.json.
  read the answers ..... python3 ~/station/app/bin/feedback.py   (remotely: station feedback)
  who reads them ....... "helper = ..." under [personal] in config.ini names them on the checklist
  hide the link ........ "checklist = no" under [display] in config.ini
Two radios ............. the radio box shows the radio in use: the one transmitting, or else the one tuned
                         (or switched on) last. With both on, the radio's name has arrows: click it to show
                         the other one. "Tune to" tunes the radio showing, or the other one if only that one
                         has an antenna for the band. The log notes which radio each contact was made on.
Connect a radio ........ ~/station/app/bin/radio.sh   (the setup helper runs it). It finds the radio's
                         Hamlib model, its cable and the speed it answers at, and saves them under [radio]
                         in config.ini. With --check it only tests the cable and changes nothing.
Second radio ........... put its name under [radio2] in config.ini, then run ~/station/app/bin/radio2.sh
                         (remotely: station radio2). It saves what it finds under [radio2].
Bands .................. the spot list's band buttons, band openings and Listen to yourself show only the
                         bands the antennas cover: "bands = ..." under [radio] and [radio2] in config.ini.
While transmitting ..... the radio box shows the power going out and the SWR. SWR turns amber from 1.5
                         and red from 2.5, and a warning pops up if it stays at 3 or more.
"Not answering" ........ the radio is probably switched off; the link reconnects when it's back on.
"Cable unplugged" ...... the radio's USB cable isn't plugged into the computer.
Alerts ................. a banner on the map for Environment Canada weather alerts near the station and
                         for NOAA space weather (solar flares, radiation and geomagnetic storms).
                         "Full warning" opens the alert in the web browser; the X hides it until it
                         changes. Thunderstorm alerts carry a reminder to disconnect the antennas.
                         The chip on Band conditions shows space weather at a glance (click for NOAA).
  settings ............. [alerts] in config.ini: weather_region (Canada only: the code at the end of your
                         area's alert page address on weather.gc.ca/warnings; blank turns weather alerts off)
                         and space_weather (yes or no)
Satellite passes ....... the tile at the bottom: the next passes of the ISS and the FM satellites (SO-50, AO-123,
                         AO-91, PO-101). Click one for where to point, its frequencies (with the Doppler steps on
                         70 cm), its notes, and AMSAT's latest reports. The sky chart is the pass: the edge is the
                         horizon, the middle is straight up, north at the top. The satellite shows on the map too.
  which ones ........... "fm = ..." under [satellites] in config.ini
Who can hear you ....... the tile at the bottom. "Where stations near you are being heard" comes from PSK Reporter:
                         FT8 and other digital signals sent from about 200 km around you, and who decoded them, in
                         the last 15 minutes. Click a band to see those stations on the map as green dots (on the
                         beam map, lines out from the middle show which way the band is open). Point at a green
                         dot to see who it is and which station near you they heard; click it to look them up.
                         Esc or the X hides them. Once you run FT8 yourself, it also says how many stations
                         decoded you.
                         DX cluster spots of your callsign are listed there too, with their paths on the map.
  switch it off ........ "enabled = no" under [openings] in config.ini (it uses 50 to 100 MB a day when busy)
Listen to yourself ..... the button on that tile: public KiwiSDR receivers a good distance away for the radio's
                         band (the ones in your beam first, if a station is looked up). "Listen" opens one in the
                         web browser, tuned to the radio's frequency. Say your call and hear yourself a second later.
Waterfall .............. with an RTL-SDR on the radio's IF and "start = yes" under [waterfall] in config.ini, the
                         band around the radio's frequency shows on the computer's own screen, centred on the dial
                         like a radio's own scope. It follows the dial, the band and the sideband by itself, and
                         reopens a few seconds after it's closed. The buttons at the top show 20 kHz up to the
                         whole band. Click a signal, or a DX spot's callsign on one, to tune there. While the radio
                         transmits, its lines show red, marked TX with the frequency (the split one when split is on).
  reopen it ............ systemctl --user restart station-waterfall
  close it for now ..... systemctl --user stop station-waterfall   (it opens again when the computer restarts)
  turn it off .......... "start = no" under [waterfall] in config.ini, then restart the app
  its messages ......... journalctl --user -u station-waterfall -n 30   (the window; the receiver's are the app's)
  on frequency ......... about once a minute it checks itself against shortwave broadcast stations (their
                         carriers sit exactly on 5 kHz steps) and corrects small drift by itself. It keeps that
                         for each of the radio's modes, since the radio can put USB and LSB a little differently.
  picture settings ..... gain, swap_iq (which way round), offset_hz and ppm under [waterfall] in config.ini.
                         The health report says what the waterfall has found out about them.
Windows ................ on the computer's own screen, whatever opens (WSJT-X, the web browser for a favourite, a
                         look-up or Listen to yourself, any other program) goes in a side area on the right, and the
                         waterfall narrows to the left to make room, so it stays whole and clickable. When nothing
                         is left beside it (closed or minimized), the waterfall takes the whole screen again.
                         Something that opens over the station display comes over too. WSJT-X's Wide Graph goes
                         along the bottom and its main window above it (all the way down while the Wide Graph is
                         closed); the side area widens a little for WSJT-X, whose Wide Graph can't be narrower than
                         about 845 pixels. Moved or resized by hand, a window stays where it's put. A program's own
                         small windows (Save as, Log QSO, settings) open over it as usual.
  wider or narrower .... side_width under [layout] in config.ini (800 pixels; never less than a window there
                         needs), then systemctl --user restart station-windows
  turn it off .......... "keep = no" under [layout] in config.ini, then systemctl --user restart station-windows
  its messages ......... journalctl --user -u station-windows -n 30
Remote listening ....... "station listen" on another computer (the Pi) opens the waterfall in its web browser with a
                         Listen button, and plays the sound from the waterfall's own receiver. Follow the radio plays
                         its frequency: both sides of a contact, your own signal too while you transmit. Where I click
                         plays any signal clicked on, in USB, LSB, AM or CW, with a fine tune. It only listens: it
                         never tunes the radio or transmits. While someone listens, the waterfall here shows "Remote
                         listener", and the health report says what they're listening to.
FT8 (WSJT-X) ........... the FT8 button at the top of the waterfall, or Menu > WSJT-X (FT8) (or type FT8 in the
                         menu's search), opens WSJT-X beside the waterfall (the button has a green light while WSJT-X
                         is open). Pressed again, the button puts WSJT-X away (minimized: it keeps listening, and the
                         map keeps filling in), and once more brings it back; if something covers WSJT-X, it brings
                         WSJT-X to the front. It hears the radio
                         through the waterfall's receiver and follows the radio's frequency: tune the radio to an FT8
                         frequency (20 m: 14.074 USB) and the stations it decodes appear in Band Activity every 15
                         seconds. They also show on the station display's map as small violet dots for 15 minutes
                         (from WSJT-X's own reports on 127.0.0.1:2237): point at one for who it is, how strong and
                         how far; click it to look them up. For now it only receives: the radio has no sound cable
                         for digital modes yet, WSJT-X's sound goes nowhere, and its link to the radio refuses
                         anything that would make the radio transmit. If WSJT-X starts a transmission (Enable Tx, or
                         a double-click on a CQ), nothing goes on the air; Halt Tx stops it. When WSJT-X opens, it
                         moves the radio's frequency a few tens of hertz and back (it's checking how finely the radio
                         tunes).
  its settings ......... the installer gives WSJT-X the station's settings the first time (File > Settings):
                         General: callsign and grid (4 characters, all FT8 sends); Radio: Rig "Hamlib NET rigctl",
                         Network Server 127.0.0.1:4534, PTT Method VOX, Mode None, Split Operation None; Audio:
                         Input station_receiver (Mono), Output station_no_transmit. Leave the Radio and Audio ones
                         as they are: the health report says if WSJT-X's radio or sound input changes.
  the clock ............ FT8 needs the computer's clock right to within a second; the health report's Clock line
                         says whether it keeps in step with internet time.
  PSK Reporter ......... WSJT-X can share the stations it hears with pskreporter.info (File > Settings > Reporting >
                         Enable PSK Reporter Spotting). It then sends this station's callsign and the grid in
                         General, so keep that to 4 characters (DO32): anything longer goes public too. The health
                         report's PSK Reporter line says whether it's on, and as whom.
  no decodes? .......... the Monitor button should be green, WSJT-X's Mode menu on FT8 and the radio on an FT8
                         frequency; WSJT-X's sound meter (bottom left) shows about 30 dB with just noise (more on a
                         busy band, where the signals add up). The health report's FT8 and Clock lines say if
                         anything is wrong on the station's side. When the radio is on an FT8 frequency and still
                         nothing comes, the band is probably just quiet: the FT8 stations line then names the band
                         the most stations near you are being heard on (from PSK Reporter), and WSJT-X's band
                         buttons (20, 10...) tune the radio to its FT8 frequency.
  turn it off .......... "enabled = no" under [ft8] in config.ini, then restart the app
  its messages ......... journalctl --user -u station-app -n 50 | grep FT8

Data sources: AD1C country file (country-files.com); DX spots from public DX cluster nodes;
band conditions from N0NBH (hamqsl.com); ISS orbits from CelesTrak; Canadian hams'
towns from ISED's call sign list (Open Government Licence - Canada) and GeoNames
postal codes (CC BY 4.0); coastlines for the globe and beam map from Natural Earth
(public domain); CQ zone list checked against EI8IC's (mapability.com); weather alerts
from Environment and Climate Change Canada (weather.gc.ca); space weather from NOAA's
Space Weather Prediction Center (spaceweather.gov); band openings from PSK Reporter
(pskreporter.info, live feed at mqtt.pskreporter.info by M0LTE); satellite orbits from
CelesTrak and satellite reports from AMSAT (amsat.org); public KiwiSDR receivers from
rx.linkfanel.net and kiwisdr.com.

From another computer (a Raspberry Pi, say), the "station" command in the app's remote folder
does most of this over SSH:
station status, station screen, station listen, station update FILE, station settings,
station restart, station radio2, station feedback, station logs, station backups,
station ssh, station host.
"station screen" shows the station display; "station screen laptop" shows the
computer's own screen (where the waterfall is); "station screen both" shows both. Each
picture is shrunk to fit the other computer's screen, so nothing is cut off.
"station listen" plays the radio's sound there (see Remote listening).
